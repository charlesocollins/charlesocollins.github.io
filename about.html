About Me

My academic journey and interest in geospatial technologies

I am Charles Olalekan Collins, a Master’s student in Applied Geoinformatics at Paris Lodron University Salzburg, Austria. My academic interests focus on using geospatial technologies to understand environmental, urban and societal challenges.

Who I Am

My work combines Geographic Information Systems (GIS), remote sensing, spatial analysis, geovisualization and spatial data management.

During my studies, I have worked on projects involving urban heat, environmental monitoring, population mapping, solar-energy potential, land degradation, spatial data infrastructure, WebGIS and accessibility analysis.

I enjoy transforming complex spatial datasets into clear maps, spatial models, dashboards and research outputs that can support researchers, planners, organisations and decision-makers.

Academic Development

My Applied Geoinformatics programme has strengthened my practical knowledge of spatial data acquisition, processing, analysis and visualization.

Through coursework and project-based learning, I have gained experience with tools and technologies such as QGIS, ArcGIS Pro, Google Earth Engine, remote sensing, spatial databases, WebGIS, network analysis and statistical analysis.

I have also developed experience in cartographic design, raster and vector analysis, spatial modelling, image interpretation, database management and the communication of geospatial results.

Research Interests

Urban Heat Climate Risk Remote Sensing Environmental Monitoring Spatial Accessibility Network Analysis Public Health Geography Spatial Planning Land-use Analysis WebGIS Spatial Data Infrastructure Geospatial Decision Support

Professional Direction

My goal is to continue developing as a geospatial professional working in areas where GIS, remote sensing, environmental research and spatial data science can contribute to practical decision-making.

I am particularly interested in opportunities related to GIS analysis, remote sensing, environmental consulting, spatial planning, geospatial research, WebGIS and spatial data management.

I see geoinformatics not only as a technical field, but also as a powerful way of understanding relationships between people, places and the environment.

My Approach: I value clear spatial communication, careful interpretation of data and the development of practical geospatial solutions that can contribute to research, planning and evidence-based decision-making.

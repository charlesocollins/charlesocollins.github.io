Master Thesis

Geospatial Analysis of Urban Heat Risk and Cooling Accessibility for Vulnerable Populations in Salzburg

The research combines climate information, satellite observations, demographic vulnerability and spatial accessibility to identify areas where heat exposure and limited cooling opportunities overlap.

Research Focus

  • Long-term climate trends and heat extremes
  • Land surface temperature patterns
  • Vegetation and built-up characteristics
  • Vulnerability of children and older adults
  • Accessibility to green and blue cooling infrastructure
  • Integrated urban heat-risk mapping

Core Methods

  • Landsat 8/9 summer composites
  • LST, NDVI and NDBI derivation
  • GIS zonal and spatial statistics
  • Network analysis and service-area modelling
  • Demographic vulnerability analysis
  • Composite heat-risk indexing

Tools & Technologies

QGISGoogle Earth EngineLandsat 8/9QNEAT3SPSSOpenStreetMap

Expected Outputs

  • Climate-trend results
  • LST, NDVI and NDBI maps
  • Vulnerability maps
  • Green/blue accessibility maps
  • Integrated urban heat-risk index
  • Priority-area recommendations
